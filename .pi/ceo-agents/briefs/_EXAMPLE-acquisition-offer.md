# Brief: Should We Accept the Acquisition Offer?

**Date Submitted:** 2026-03-24
**Submitted By:** CEO
**Decision Needed By:** 2026-03-31
**Board Topic:** Strategic

---

## Situation

We've received an acquisition offer from TechCorp (valued at $50M) for CleanExpo. They want to acquire our technology, team, and customer base. This is the first serious acquisition inquiry we've received in 3 years.

TechCorp is a well-established player in adjacent market (enterprise software). They've indicated they want to integrate our platform with their ecosystem and expand our go-to-market reach. The offer comes at a time when:
- We're growing at 40% YoY
- We have 2 years of runway remaining
- Our Series B process has stalled (only 1 meeting this quarter)
- Competitive pressure is increasing in our segment

The offer is attractive but also raises strategic questions. Walking away means continuing the capital raise journey and accepting execution risk. Accepting means uncertainty about our autonomy and long-term vision.

## Stakes

**Downside if we choose poorly:**
- If we sell and integrate poorly: team attrition, product dilution, loss of strategic control, customer churn
- If we decline and funding dries up: forced down-round, runway exhaustion, being forced to sell on worse terms
- If we decline and market shifts: competitors pull ahead, our advantages erode, valuation drops

**Upside if we choose well:**
- If we sell to right acquirer: team security, continued growth without funding pressure, expanded market access, potential earnout upside
- If we decline and fund successfully: complete control, ability to build our vision, potential for 10x+ exit later
- If we decline and master execution: become category leader, self-sufficient, maximize shareholder value

## Constraints

- **Timeline:** Must decide within 1 week (TechCorp has other conversations)
- **Financing:** Series B at same valuation unlikely; next round would be down-round
- **Team:** 12 employees, mostly early-career; acquisition uncertainty may trigger departures
- **Market:** Consolidation accelerating in our segment; independent players becoming rare
- **Customer:** 50% of revenue from enterprise customers who value our independence

## Key Questions for the Board

1. **Valuation Reality Check** — Is $50M the right price for where we are and could go? What's the real comparison?
2. **Founder Motivation** — What do we actually want? Money? Control? Impact? Market dominance?
3. **Integration Risk** — How risky is this acquisition culturally and strategically?
4. **Market Timing** — Is this a window closing or opening? Will better opportunities emerge?
5. **Path to Independence** — If we say no, can we realistically fund and scale alone?

## Background & Supporting Context

**Current Metrics:**
- MRR: $180k (growing 4% month-over-month)
- ARR: $2.16M
- Customer Count: 34 enterprise customers
- Churn: 2% annually (very low)
- Runway: 24 months with current burn
- Team: 12 FTE (8 engineers, 2 sales, 2 ops)

**Market Context:**
- Total addressable market (TAM) for our category: $500M and growing
- Competitive landscape: 3 major players (all well-funded), 20+ startups
- Industry consolidation: 8 acquisitions in space in last 18 months
- Customer sentiment: High satisfaction (NPS 65), value independence

**Prior Context:**
- Series A (2024): $4M at $15M valuation
- Profitability: Currently 6 months from breakeven
- Strategic partnerships: In talks with 2 potential integrations

## Proposed Options

### Option A: Accept the Offer

**Pros:**
- Eliminates funding uncertainty and runway pressure
- Provides team with security and liquidity
- Unlocks TechCorp's distribution and resources
- Proven acquirer in adjacent space
- Clear exit for founders and early employees

**Cons:**
- Loss of independence and strategic control
- Potential for cultural misalignment
- Risk of team attrition post-acquisition
- May undervalue long-term potential (10x upside?)
- Customers who value independence may churn
- No control over product direction post-deal

### Option B: Counter-Offer & Negotiate

**Pros:**
- Demonstrates openness while improving terms
- Buys time for Series B conversations
- Tests TechCorp's true valuation ceiling
- Maintains optionality

**Cons:**
- May offend TechCorp (they could walk)
- Doesn't address core strategic question
- Just delays the decision

### Option C: Decline & Pursue Series B

**Pros:**
- Maintains full control and strategic autonomy
- Team stays excited and unified
- Customers who value independence stay loyal
- Potential for much larger exit in 3-5 years
- Prove we can grow without external capital

**Cons:**
- Continuing capital raise is hard and distracting
- Next round could be down-round if market shifts
- Execution risk increases without runway buffer
- Team may leave for more stable opportunity
- Competitive pressure could erode position

## Note to the Board

This is a real strategic crossroads. I don't have a predetermined answer—I genuinely want the board's perspective on what we should do. The financial case could go either way depending on your views on market timing and our execution capability.

I'm particularly interested in:
- The Compounder's view on long-term upside
- The Contrarian's view on hidden risks in the deal
- The Market Strategist's read on competitive window
- The CEO's synthesis of all perspectives
